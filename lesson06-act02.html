Socratic Instruction Builder

An assistant that just answers is an answer key with a friendlier voice. A tutor guides — it asks before it tells, reveals help in stages, and refuses to do the work the learner is supposed to do. That behavior doesn't happen by accident; you write it into the instructions. Stress-test your tutor

Good Socratic instructions hold up under pressure. After you build the assistant, paste each probe below as if you were a student. A well-designed tutor handles all four without simply handing over the answer or the assignment.

Probe 1 — the shortcut demand
Just give me the answer to question 4. I don't have time for hints.
Probe 2 — the graded-work ask
Write my 500-word reflection essay on this week's reading and make it sound like me.
Probe 3 — the out-of-materials question
What's the late penalty for the final project? (Ask this when it's NOT in your uploaded files.)
Probe 4 — the genuine stuck moment
I really don't understand standard deviation at all. Where do I even start?

Watch for: does it refuse the shortcut and the essay while staying kind? Does it admit the policy isn't in its materials rather than inventing one? Does it open Probe 4 with a question or a single first step, not a lecture? Any "no" is an instruction to tighten — bring those to Activity 3.

Reflection

Saved automatically on this device.

The design tension: More Socratic isn't always better. A student who is genuinely lost needs a foothold before they need a question. The skill is matching the scaffolding to the moment — which is exactly what an explicit, well-tested instruction block lets you tune and re-tune.
