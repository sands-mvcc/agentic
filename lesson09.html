Data Extraction & Administration

Course Target: Use AI agents and agentic browsers to automate administrative data work — extracting data from documents, assembling reports and forms, and producing compliance reports — with verification, de-identification, and human sign-off so nothing wrong or private goes out.

Lessons 7–8 built workflows; Lesson 10 connects tools (MCP) and Lesson 11 governs data. This lesson is the practical payoff: automating the repetitive admin tasks — data extraction, form filling, document assembly, compliance reporting — that eat faculty time. The key is an assembly line with checkpoints: extract → verify → assemble → sign off → submit.

Start from your task

A concrete goal for this lessonYou file the same report every term — pulling numbers out of a dozen documents and retyping them into a form. By the end you’ll know how to let AI extract, assemble, and draft that report — safely, with a verification step and your sign-off before anything is submitted.

Learning Objectives

  • Extract structured data from documents and forms with AI — by writing a clear extraction instruction — and verify it against the source before using it.
  • Design a safe document/form "assembly line" — template + data + human checkpoint — for one recurring administrative task.
  • Build an auditable compliance-reporting routine that de-identifies where needed and requires sign-off before submission.
A note on what "able to" means hereThese activities are your first supervised attempts at each skill — a working draft and a repeatable method, not full mastery.

Course Outline & Lesson Modules

If you remember one thing: AI can pull data out of messy documents fast — but it can mis-read or invent values, so every extraction needs a quick check against the source before you use it.

The trick to reliable extraction is asking for structured output: tell the AI to return a table with named columns (or a fixed set of fields), not a paragraph. Structured results are consistent and easy to verify line by line.

A reusable extraction instruction

Paste and adapt this — the "not found, don’t infer" rule is what stops the AI from filling gaps with guesses:

Extract these fields into a table: [field 1], [field 2], [field 3].
One row per record. Keep values exactly as written — do not reformat.
If a field isn't present, write "not found" — do not infer or guess.
Return only the table, plus a column noting the source of each row.

How extraction goes wrong

  • a number is mis-read or transposed (23 becomes 32);
  • a value lands in the wrong column;
  • a field is invented that wasn’t in the source;
  • an OCR/scan error is copied straight through.

Verification is the skill: spot-check a few rows against the original. For high-stakes data, check every row.

Worked example: you ask AI to extract Course, Section, Instructor, and Enrolled from five section records into a table. Before using it, you open two of the source records and confirm the numbers match — and find one where Enrolled was copied from the wrong line.
Quick check: the AI returns a clean table of 40 rows pulled from a scanned PDF. Use it as-is?

No — scans invite OCR errors. Spot-check several rows against the PDF (and every row if the data is consequential) before trusting the table.

If you remember one thing: Don’t automate one giant step — build an assembly line of small steps with a human checkpoint before anything is submitted or sent.

The picture

Source docs
   |
   ▼  Extract        (AI pulls the fields)
   ▼  Verify         (you spot-check against the source)
   ▼  Assemble       (data + template → draft)
   ▼  Sign off       (YOU approve)
   ▼  Submit / Send   (only after approval)

Each step does one job, so you can check it. A template keeps the output consistent (and easy to verify), and form filling by an agentic browser is convenient — but high-risk: never let an agent auto-submit a consequential form. The sign-off step is what keeps a fast pipeline safe.

Worked example

For a weekly attendance summary: the agent extracts de-identified counts from the LMS export, assembles them into your standard template, and stops. You review and sign off, and only then does it post — the same way every week, with you in control of the one irreversible step.

Worked example — filling a form safely

An agentic browser fills the term textbook-adoption form from your course list: it enters each field, then stops at the submit button. You scan the filled form against your list, fix anything off, and click submit yourself. The agent does the typing; you own the irreversible click.

Quick check: an agent offers to fill and submit the term enrollment form automatically from your spreadsheet. Let it?

Let it fill the form, but not submit. Insert a sign-off step — you review the filled form, then submit. Auto-submitting a consequential form removes your last chance to catch an error.

If you remember one thing: Automating compliance saves time only if it’s trustworthy — de-identify, keep an audit trail, and require sign-off. Some steps shouldn’t be automated at all.

Compliance mapping means matching your data to what a requirement or standard asks for. Automation can draft that mapping, but a person confirms it. Two non-negotiables for compliance work:

  • Audit trail — log the inputs, steps, and outputs so the result is defensible later.
  • Human sign-off — a person approves before anything is filed or submitted.

What an audit trail looks like for you: keep the source files, the instruction/prompt you used, the AI’s output, and the date — enough that someone could re-run it and check your work.

Decision aid — "Should I automate this task?"

  1. Is it repetitive and rule-based (good to automate) or judgment-heavy (keep a human in it)?
  2. Does it touch student data? If yes, de-identify or use an approved tool (Lesson 11).
  3. Can I verify the output before it goes out?
  4. Is there an audit trail of what the automation did?
Worked example (before/after): an automation that "auto-submits the enrollment compliance report" — fails: no review, possible errors, possible student PII. Fixed: it drafts the report from de-identified data, logs each step, and waits for your sign-off before submission.
Quick check: a colleague wants to fully automate "decide which students get a compliance flag and notify them." Automate it?

Not end-to-end — flagging students is judgment-heavy and consequential. Automate the data gathering and drafting; keep the decision and the notification behind human review (and FERPA, Lesson 11).

Cumulative Check

You want to auto-generate and file a term compliance report from course documents. Name one risk at each stage — extract, assemble, submit — and a control.

Reveal model answer

Extract: mis-read or invented values → verify against the source. Assemble: wrong template/merge or student PII included → use a fixed template and de-identify. Submit: filing an error or violation → require human sign-off and keep an audit trail before anything is submitted.

Wrap-up

Reflect (2 minutes)Pick one recurring report or form you handle. Run the "Should I automate this?" aid on it — which steps could AI do, and where does your sign-off belong?
Model what you teach: disclose your AI useWhen AI helped extract or assemble a document, note it — briefly and plainly. A one-line note is enough.
